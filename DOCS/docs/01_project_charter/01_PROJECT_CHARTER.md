# Investment Intelligence OS
## Project Charter — v0.1

**Status:** Approved foundation for Version 1 build  
**Build date:** August 20, 2026  
**Owner:** Founder / Single Operator  
**V1 operating mode:** Research, backtesting, event studies, scenario analysis, and paper trading  
**Live-money automation:** Disabled  
**North Star:** Maximize decision quality under uncertainty. A compelling story is not a validated edge.

---

## 1. Executive Mandate

The Investment Intelligence OS (IIOS) is a personal-first, institution-ready investment research and paper-trading platform.

IIOS will convert lawful public information into testable, explainable, risk-controlled investment decisions through continuous learning.

It is not intended to be a headline-reacting bot that buys or sells because a public figure, market commentator, investor, executive, technical indicator, or AI model made a compelling statement. It is intended to build and maintain a structured model of the economic world, identify possible causal transmission into financial markets, test competing explanations, estimate uncertainty, control portfolio risk, simulate execution, and learn from outcomes.

The system will treat presidential behavior, executive actions, White House meetings, congressional activity, Federal Reserve decisions, wars, sanctions, neighboring-country policy, weather, agriculture, livestock disease, supply chains, commodities, public investor disclosures, corporate filings, market structure, and historical regimes as interacting components of one economic ecosystem.

No component is automatically trusted. Every proposed edge must be earned through evidence, point-in-time testing, forward paper performance, and disciplined risk control.

## 2. Problem Statement

Markets react to a large number of interconnected signals:

- policy intent;
- formal policy implementation;
- monetary conditions;
- legislative probability;
- court actions;
- global conflict;
- sanctions and trade restrictions;
- weather and natural disasters;
- agricultural and livestock conditions;
- commodity supply and demand;
- corporate investment and guidance;
- public ownership and flow disclosures;
- technical and market-structure conditions;
- investor expectations;
- valuation;
- liquidity;
- positioning;
- historical regime context.

A human operator cannot reliably monitor, normalize, connect, test, and remember all of these signals at once.

Most retail trading tools solve only a small part of the problem. They may screen charts, summarize news, copy public trades, or execute predefined rules, but they rarely preserve a complete chain from source to evidence to causal thesis to risk decision to outcome.

IIOS exists to close that gap.

## 3. Mission

## 4. Vision

Build a personal investment-intelligence platform that helps one operator develop institutional-quality judgment, then preserve the modularity, provenance, security, auditability, and governance required to scale into an institutional product later.

The long-term system should be capable of supporting:

- multiple users and roles;
- multiple asset classes;
- licensed data providers;
- model and prompt governance;
- broker integrations;
- compliance review;
- research collaboration;
- institutional audit requirements;
- controlled promotion from research to paper trading to limited live use.

V1 will not attempt to deliver the entire institutional product. It will prove the core reasoning and learning loop.

---

## 5. Investment Philosophy

IIOS is governed by the following investment posture:

1. **Observe before predicting.**
2. **Understand before recommending.**
3. **Test before trusting.**
4. **Risk before execution.**
5. **Learn after every decision.**
6. **No-trade is a valid and often superior decision.**
7. **Capital protection outranks excitement.**
8. **Complexity must prove that it adds value.**
9. **Confidence is measured and calibrated, not declared.**
10. **A profitable outcome does not automatically mean the reasoning was sound.**
11. **A losing outcome does not automatically mean the decision process was wrong.**
12. **Every material belief must be revisable when evidence changes.**

## 9. Intelligence Domains

IIOS will maintain distinct but connected intelligence domains.

| Domain | What It Watches | Example Market Transmission |
|---|---|---|
| Presidency and policy | Executive orders, memoranda, proclamations, remarks, meetings, industrial policy, tariffs, contracting priorities | Policy → regulation, capex, trade, or contracting → company and sector expectations |
| Congress and regulation | Bills, amendments, committees, votes, agencies, implementation | Legislative probability → legal or sector exposure → valuation and capital-allocation effects |
| Federal Reserve, rates, and macro | FOMC, speeches, inflation, labor, growth, liquidity, yield curve, dollar | Rates and liquidity → discount rates, credit, currency, equities, commodities |
| Global and geopolitical | War, sanctions, trade, diplomacy, shipping lanes, country policy | Conflict or trade disruption → supply, logistics, risk premium → commodities, FX, sectors |
| Weather and climate | Hurricanes, drought, rain, temperature, flood, freeze, anomalies | Weather → crops, energy, transport, insurance → supply-demand and margins |
| Agriculture and livestock | Crop calendars, yields, disease, herd size, inventories, substitution | Supply shock and seasonality → spot and futures prices → producers, processors, restaurants |
| Commodities | Energy, metals, agriculture, inventories, curves, substitution | Input availability and cost → margins, inflation, substitutions, downstream sectors |
| Corporate and sector | Filings, capex, guidance, earnings, customers, suppliers, peers, valuation | Fundamentals and exposure → earnings revisions, valuation, sector rotation |
| Public flows | Public 13F, insider filings, CFTC reports, fund flows | Lagged ownership and positioning → crowding and context, not automatic copy trades |
| Market structure | Price, trend, breadth, volatility, options, futures, liquidity | Confirmation, timing, entry, sizing, and invalidation context |
| Strategy research | Public strategies, academic research, disclosed trades, observable bot behavior | Observed behavior → strategy hypothesis → historical test → candidate module |

## 10. Presidential and Policy Intelligence Mandate

Presidential behavior and federal policy are signature components of IIOS, but they are not unquestioned oracles.

The policy system must distinguish among:

- rhetoric or public remark;
- announced intent;
- formal executive action;
- proposed rule or legislation;
- signed law;
- implementation step;
- judicial challenge;
- delayed, limited, or reversed action;
- realized economic effect.

A public meeting between a government official and a company or executive is evidence that a meeting occurred. It is not proof of preferential treatment, future contracts, future investment returns, or a tradable edge.

Every policy-derived thesis must identify:

- implementation authority;
- likely timeline;
- beneficiaries and harmed parties;
- second-order effects;
- legal and political constraints;
- opposing interpretation;
- market expectations already reflected in price;
- evidence that would falsify the thesis.

---

## 11. Public Strategy and Trading-Bot Research Mandate

IIOS may study publicly disclosed investment strategies, portfolio holdings, trade records, academic research, fund letters, public interviews, and observable trading-bot behavior.

The system may:

- identify repeated behavior;
- estimate likely strategy family;
- infer possible entry, exit, sizing, or risk patterns;
- reconstruct a testable hypothesis;
- compare the hypothesis against alternative explanations;
- backtest and forward-test the hypothesis;
- add a validated strategy module to the research library.

The system must not claim it has recovered an undisclosed algorithm exactly unless the algorithm has actually been disclosed and the claim can be verified.

Observed behavior becomes a hypothesis, not a copy.

## 12. Operating Flow

Every decision must pass through seven stages:

1. **Observe —** What happened, when, and from what source?
2. **Understand —** What entities, relationships, sectors, countries, commodities, and assets are implicated?
3. **Reason —** How could the event transmit into markets, what lags are expected, and what contradicts the chain?
4. **Decide —** Is there a thesis worth advancing?
5. **Risk —** How much risk, if any, is appropriate?
6. **Simulate —** What would historical and paper execution look like after realistic costs?
7. **Learn —** What was right, wrong, missing, lucky, or misunderstood?

No stage may be skipped because a narrative feels obvious.

## 15. Out of Scope for V1

- Autonomous live-money execution
- Guaranteed return targets
- Treating a $10,000-to-$1,000,000 aspiration as a software acceptance criterion
- Use of material nonpublic information
- Use of hacked, leaked, stolen, confidential, or improperly licensed data
- High-frequency trading

- Unreviewed legal, tax, or regulatory conclusions
- Public customer onboarding

## 16. Success Criteria

V1 is successful when the system can repeatedly:

1. ingest multiple official or licensed sources;
2. preserve what the market could know at the time;
3. trace a recommendation to immutable evidence;
4. separate fact, inference, hypothesis, and decision;
5. generate a causal chain and credible counter-chain;
6. identify missing information;
7. preserve disagreement among agents;
8. produce a committee decision or no-trade decision;
9. apply risk limits and vetoes;
10. simulate realistic paper execution;
11. journal the complete reasoning chain;
12. compare outcomes with benchmarks;
13. update beliefs and retire failed hypotheses;
14. stand down safely when data, models, or operations are unreliable.

## 17. Governance and Decision Rights

| Role | Authority |
|---|---|
| Founder / Product Owner | Sets objectives, approves scope, accepts major trade-offs, and authorizes promotions between operating modes |
| AI Architect | Designs system boundaries, schemas, interfaces, tests, and technical controls |
| Specialist Agents | Produce bounded analyses with evidence and uncertainty; cannot place orders |
| Investment Committee | Aggregates evidence, preserves dissent, and issues candidate or no-trade decisions |
| Risk Manager / Risk Engine | Sizes, reduces, rejects, or vetoes any candidate |
| Paper Execution Engine | Simulates approved actions only; does not create theses |
| Human Operator | Reviews and approves any transition beyond paper mode |
| Future Compliance / Legal Review | Required before institutional or live deployment where applicable |

No AI component owns final accountability.

## 18. Constraints

- One operator in V1
- Four-week target for a useful personal-production prototype
- Seven-day target for the first end-to-end vertical slice
- Public or properly licensed information only
- Point-in-time integrity
- Limited initial budget and implementation capacity
- Vendor-neutral architecture
- Auditability from the first release
- Paper mode until explicit promotion
- Risk engine may always choose no-trade
- Complexity must be justified by measurable value

---

## 19. Principal Risks

The project is most likely to fail through:

- false causality;
- political or narrative confirmation bias;
- look-ahead leakage;
- overfitting;
- stale or low-quality data;
- disclosure-lag errors;
- unrealistic execution assumptions;
- hidden concentration;
- model hallucination;
- weak auditability;
- operational or credential failure;
- uncontrolled scope growth;
- confusing high confidence with high expected return.

The Risk Register owns detailed mitigations and review status.

## 20. Delivery Milestones

### Seven-Day Vertical Slice

| Day | Focus | Definition of Success |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Repository, charter, constitution, configuration, schemas, tests, registers | Codebase runs locally and the paper-only boundary is explicit |
| 2 | Official-source ingestion | White House or policy, Fed or macro, and one non-policy source normalize into canonical events |
| 3 | World model, evidence, causality | One event produces evidence links, entities, causal chain, counter-chain, and missing-information list |
| 4 | Agents and committee | Policy, macro, and skeptic views produce a structured candidate or no-trade decision |
| 5 | Risk and paper execution | Approved candidate becomes a risk-reviewed simulated order and position |
| 6 | Backtest, event study, dashboard | Historical test and paper state appear in a minimal command center |
| 7 | Audit demonstration | One complete source-to-decision-to-paper-trade trace is reconstructable and tested |

### Four-Week V1

- **Week 1 — Prove the loop:** Minimal end-to-end vertical slice.
- **Week 2 — Broaden the world model:** Congress, SEC, NOAA, USDA, EIA, sanctions, trade, entity resolution, source health, decision journal.
- **Week 3 — Prove research discipline:** Point-in-time datasets, event studies, baselines, strategy reconstruction, walk-forward tests, costs, regime evaluation.
- **Week 4 — Harden personal production:** Scheduler, daily brief, command center, risk dashboard, paper portfolio, model registry, audit log, backup and recovery.

## 21. Capital Objective

The system may support ambitious capital-growth goals, but it may not represent them as guaranteed, expected, or required outcomes.

Capital growth is an outcome to earn through:

- genuine edge;
- disciplined position sizing;
- controlled drawdowns;
- realistic execution;
- repeatability;
- favorable asymmetry;
- long-term learning.

The software requirement is decision quality and risk discipline, not a predetermined wealth outcome.
