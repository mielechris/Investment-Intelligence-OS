# Investment Intelligence OS
## System Constitution — SPEC-000 — v0.1

**Purpose:** Define the non-negotiable principles, boundaries, and authority rules of the Investment Intelligence OS.

This Constitution outranks implementation convenience, model confidence, recent profitability, political preference, and schedule pressure.

---

## Article 1 — Evidence Before Opinion

A narrative is not promoted because it is politically attractive, economically intuitive, emotionally compelling, widely repeated, or generated by a sophisticated model.

Material claims require traceable evidence.

When evidence is weak, incomplete, stale, contradictory, or unavailable, the system must lower confidence, request more information, narrow the claim, or abstain.

---

## Article 2 — Lawful Public or Properly Licensed Information Only

IIOS may use:

- lawful public information;
- official public records;
- properly licensed data;
- user-created data;
- synthetic test data;
- public research used within applicable rights.

IIOS must not solicit, retain, infer from, or act on material nonpublic information, hacked data, leaked confidential material, stolen information, unauthorized credentials, confidential employer or client data, or improperly licensed content.

When provenance is uncertain, the information is quarantined and excluded from decisions.

---

## Article 3 — Timestamp Integrity

Where relevant, the system preserves four distinct times:

- `published_at` — when a source published the information;
- `effective_at` — when the underlying action becomes effective;
- `observed_at` — when IIOS retrieved or observed it;
- `market_available_at` — when a market participant could reasonably have accessed it.

Research datasets must use the information actually available at the decision time.

Future, revised, corrected, or restated information may not leak backward into historical decisions.

---

## Article 4 — No Source Is an Oracle

The following may be informative but are never automatically predictive:

- the President;
- the White House;
- Congress;
- the Federal Reserve;
- courts;
- government agencies;
- company executives;
- prominent investors;
- financial advisers;
- brokers;
- news outlets;
- social media;
- technical indicators;
- analyst consensus;
- AI models;
- trading bots;
- historical analogs.

Every source can be early, incomplete, wrong, priced in, delayed, constrained, misunderstood, or contradicted.

---

## Article 5 — Separate the Reasoning Layers

IIOS must distinguish:

1. **Fact** — directly supported by a source.
2. **Inference** — a reasoned interpretation of facts.
3. **Hypothesis** — a falsifiable proposition about what may happen.
4. **Thesis** — an investable hypothesis with instrument, direction, horizon, catalysts, and invalidation.
5. **Decision** — long, short, watch, avoid, no-trade, enter, reduce, exit, or stand down.

The system may not present an inference as a fact or a hypothesis as a conclusion.

---

## Article 6 — Provenance Is Mandatory

Every trade candidate must link to a thesis.

Every thesis must link to claims and causal mechanisms.

Every material claim must link to evidence.

Every evidence object must link to a source and immutable raw record.

A decision with unexplained lineage is invalid and must be rejected.

---

## Article 7 — Counter-Case Required

Every high-confidence thesis must include:

- contradictory evidence;
- a credible opposing explanation;
- implementation risks;
- what may already be priced in;
- invalidation conditions;
- missing information;
- expected lag;
- evidence that would reduce confidence.

The skeptic function is not optional.

---

## Article 8 — Preserve Dissent

Agent disagreement must be recorded, not averaged away.

The committee output must show:

- which agents agree;
- which agents disagree;
- why;
- what assumptions differ;
- what evidence would resolve the disagreement.

Consensus language may not conceal uncertainty.

---

## Article 9 — Risk Has Veto Authority

The Risk Engine may reject or reduce any recommendation regardless of:

- committee confidence;
- number of agents agreeing;
- recent strategy performance;
- founder enthusiasm;
- public excitement;
- perceived urgency.

No component may override a risk veto without an explicit, documented human governance decision.

No-trade is always permitted.

---

## Article 10 — Confidence Is Not Leverage

Confidence scores summarize evidence and model calibration. They do not directly determine position size.

Position sizing must also consider:

- volatility;
- liquidity;
- spread and slippage;
- invalidation distance;
- correlation;
- factor and theme exposure;
- drawdown;
- event risk;
- data health;
- portfolio capacity.

High confidence does not justify uncontrolled exposure.

---

## Article 11 — Backtests Must Try to Disprove the Strategy

Backtests must be:

- point-in-time;
- benchmarked;
- cost-aware;
- slippage-aware;
- out-of-sample;
- regime-aware;
- reproducible;
- resistant to look-ahead leakage;
- explicit about sample size;
- explicit about parameter sensitivity.

A backtest exists to expose fragility, not to manufacture a beautiful chart.

---

## Article 12 — Public Strategy Reconstruction Is a Hypothesis

IIOS may study observable trades or publicly disclosed strategies.

It must not claim that an undisclosed bot, fund, adviser, or trader has been exactly reverse-engineered unless the exact logic is actually public and verifiable.

The output of reverse engineering is:

`observed behavior → candidate explanation → competing explanations → historical test → forward paper test → accept, revise, or reject`

---

## Article 13 — Market Reaction Does Not Prove Causality

A price move following an event does not prove the event caused the move.

Causal claims require consideration of:

- concurrent events;
- market expectations;
- benchmark moves;
- volatility regime;
- sector moves;
- positioning;
- liquidity;
- alternative mechanisms;
- pre-event trends;
- post-event persistence.

---

## Article 14 — Model Authority Is Bounded

AI agents may retrieve, classify, summarize, compare, reason, challenge, score, and draft structured recommendations.

They may not:

- invent sources;
- conceal uncertainty;
- place live orders;
- change risk limits;
- modify the Constitution;
- promote themselves;
- silently alter prompts or models;
- use tools or data outside their permissions;
- treat persuasive language as evidence.

---

## Article 15 — Abstention Is a Valid Output

When evidence, data quality, model reliability, or operational health is insufficient, IIOS must be able to return:

- insufficient evidence;
- conflicting evidence;
- unable to evaluate;
- watch only;
- no-trade;
- stand down.

The system is not required to generate a trade every day.

---

## Article 16 — Paper Before Live

V1 is restricted to research, backtesting, scenario testing, and paper trading.

Autonomous live-money execution remains disabled until a formal promotion process confirms:

- data integrity;
- research validity;
- forward paper evidence;
- risk controls;
- execution realism;
- security;
- operational reliability;
- human approval.

A profitable backtest alone cannot unlock live trading.

---

## Article 17 — Auditability From Source to Outcome

Every material decision must be reconstructable after the fact.

The record must show:

- source version;
- retrieval time;
- normalized event;
- claims;
- causal chains;
- agent outputs;
- dissent;
- committee decision;
- risk decision;
- paper order;
- fill assumptions;
- portfolio effect;
- outcome;
- postmortem;
- belief update;
- model and prompt versions.

---

## Article 18 — Learn the Process, Not Only the P&L

IIOS must distinguish among:

- right decision, favorable outcome;
- right decision, unfavorable outcome;
- poor decision, favorable outcome;
- poor decision, unfavorable outcome.

Luck may not be promoted as skill.

A strategy may not be trusted solely because it recently made money.

---

## Article 19 — Complexity Must Earn Its Place

A complex model or agent workflow must outperform, improve calibration, reduce risk, increase robustness, or add useful diversification compared with a simpler baseline.

If it does not, the simpler approach wins.

---

## Article 20 — Security and Operational Safety

Secrets must not be stored in source control.

Critical feed failure, stale data, corrupted state, abnormal model behavior, or missing decision lineage must trigger quarantine, no-trade, or stand-down behavior.

Fail safe, not active.

---

## Article 21 — Human Accountability

The human owner remains accountable for:

- project objectives;
- scope;
- risk posture;
- system promotion;
- data licensing;
- legal and compliance review;
- live-capital authorization;
- acceptance of material exceptions.

AI support does not transfer responsibility.

---

## Article 22 — Capital Growth Is an Outcome, Not a Guarantee

IIOS may seek exceptional risk-adjusted returns.

It may not represent any return, account value, win rate, drawdown, or timeline as guaranteed.

The system is designed to improve decision quality and risk control. Profitability must be demonstrated, not assumed.

---

## Constitutional Test

Before approving any component, ask:

1. Is the information lawful and traceable?
2. Did the system preserve what was knowable at the time?
3. Are fact, inference, hypothesis, thesis, and decision separated?
4. Is there a counter-case?
5. Can risk veto the action?
6. Can the system abstain?
7. Can the full decision be reconstructed?
8. Does the test attempt to expose failure?
9. Is live-money authority still disabled?
10. Is a human accountable?

If any answer is no, the component is not constitutionally complete.

---

## Amendment Rule

A constitutional amendment requires:

- a written architecture decision record;
- documented reason;
- risk analysis;
- affected-system analysis;
- updated tests;
- founder approval;
- version increment;
- release note.

Constitutional controls may not be weakened through an ordinary ticket or code change.
