## Schedule and authority

`request_plan()` reproduces all 50 reviewed rows, including proposal hashes, from
specification SHA-256 `c3d969a576630a3e55ba1fa6f619147c53c78489d4ca327a72da114c129d057c`.
Ten instruments: MU, SPY, XLK, VNQ, TLT, GLD, UUP, IBIT, PFF, BIL. Five batches begin
06:30, 06:32, 06:34, 09:30 and 13:00 PDT, six seconds between nominal requests.
The count is 30 snapshots, 19 histories and one MU facts request.

Same-day approval must precede opening, have a maximum 1800-second arming interval,
and explicitly expire at 13:05 PDT / 20:05 UTC. Every dispatch verifies independently
expected account, owner and release hashes. No next-day, old-session, naive-time,
future or expired grant is valid. Runtime dispatch does not reapply the short arming
interval, so a valid pre-open grant can cover closing. Initial arming after opening
is rejected. An already armed process that misses opening deadlines records MISSED
rows and PARTIAL_SESSION; it cannot backfill them. Supporting a newly authorized
late-start grant would require another explicit policy review.

The loop ticks once per second, dispatches at most one request at a time, observes
10 requests per rolling 60 seconds, and reserves each one before credentials or
network activity. Nominal lateness is recorded. No retries, pagination, redirects,
canaries or extra certification calls are permitted. A request requires 20 seconds
of remaining window; the total transport alarm is at most 15 seconds. The last
20 seconds are conservatively unavailable for starting new work; the reviewed
hard deadlines and nominal times are unchanged. OS timer delivery and host scheduling
must still be verified in later operational testing.

The evidence classification is COMPLETE_SCHEDULED_COLLECTION only when all scheduled
rows are observed and no snapshot is stale. This does not mean continuous full-day
coverage, official auction prices, an official closing bar, or trading authority.
Historical bars retain their returned dates; company facts remain metadata.

## Failure, audit and rollback

Receipts are exclusive-create, fsynced, sequential and parent-hash chained from the
expected release hash. Raw successful responses are separately hashed and rechecked
on restart. A durable reservation with no outcome is ambiguous and terminal; no
retry occurs. TLS/account/authority/runtime/response failure revokes further spending.
Only controlled failure categories are persisted, never exception text or credential
headers. CLI failures return exit 78 and structured failure status, not success.

Rollback is disarm-first and preservation-first: write the stop marker, allow bounded
shutdown, retain the entire new root and receipts, and remove no evidence. No old
release or September 9 authority is automatically restored. A future authorized
LaunchAgent unload must verify its exact label/plist identity; it is not implemented
as a broad service-control operation. No installer rollback can touch existing roots.

fabricated production value. The unit tests use explicitly synthetic inputs only.

commit and verify the Python runtime distribution/dependencies, not merely the

account attestation is truthful. Independent review supplies that trust boundary.
